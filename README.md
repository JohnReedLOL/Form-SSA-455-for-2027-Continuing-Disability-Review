No. Again, it's not that I don't want to go back to university, it's just that I have issues with certain cognitive functions that would result in me failing every class. I want to add something about my cognitive issues. I have multiple cognitive issues. The one I talked about in the prior paragraph was concentration/attention, but there are others. For example, I have deficiencies in certain forms of memory. My phone has a “find my phone” feature and it attaches to my wallet with a keychain because without those things, I would constantly lose or misplace my wallet. I struggle with another form of memory that is used for things like real-world navigation, rote memorization, and finding/recalling my way around a body of computer code (I used to be a computer programmer, a software engineer). As far as my capacity for work goes, the cognitive issues are serious enough to make me unable to work in and of themselves.

Also, I want to address a misconception that you may have. You see my writing here. I am able to write, but my ability to read is very limited (as I mentioned two paragraphs prior). You may have recordings of me talking on the phone with Social Security. Talking can be difficult for me at times as I sometimes have to rehearse what I'm going to say or I may not be able to find the words. Like I anticipate questions that other people will ask me and I rehearse answering those questions before talking to them because coming up with spoken words impromptu can be difficult for me. That being said, even with that difficulty, speaking is much, much, much easier for me than listening for an extended period of time. Like I mentioned two paragraphs prior, I have limitations in concentration and attention.

I might be wasting extra unnecessary time explaining this, but I just wanted to add something. I have been told before “Oh look, you can write, therefore you must also be able to do X, Y, and Z”. That is wrong because the functions of the brain are much more fine-grained than that. For example, it's possible for a person to lose the ability to speak but retain the ability to listen because those are two separate brain areas. I have also been told something like “Oh, you write and speak so clearly, your other brain functions must be good, you can't have disabling cognitive issues”. Wrong. I hate hearing things like that because it undermines my lived experience. One time I recalled something from years ago and my psychiatrist APRN Edgar Matamoros wrote down in the medical records “memory grossly intact”. It should have said “LONG TERM memory grossly intact”. Long term memory is just one form of memory, and it is possible for one form of memory (like long-term memory) to be “grossly intact” while another form of memory is not. On a related note, just because something is in some medical record or some Social Security records doesn't necessarily mean it's 100% true and 100% accurate. For example, I've seen a doctor refer to me in the medical records as a “she” despite the fact that I am a straight man with a beard and a penis. There are all sorts of mistakes like that. That's all.
